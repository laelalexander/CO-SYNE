Why it mattersThe first sixty seconds of a stop

A traffic stop is the most common way residents come into contact with police, and it is where trust is won or lost.

With CO-SYNE, the first sixty seconds change:

  • The driver knows the officer is real.
  • The officer knows who is in the car, including any accessibility needs.
  • Either side can talk first, by video or by text.
  • Nothing can be quietly changed afterwards.

What communities gain

  • Safer stops for drivers and officers.
  • Trust you can show, not just promise.
  • Fairness and accessibility for drivers who are Deaf, have a disability, or speak another language.
  • Faster help with crash, medical and "I'm in danger" alerts.
  • Accountability without new surveillance.

We present this as a pilot being measured, not a proven outcome. The evaluation will tell us what works.

MessageThe core story

At every stop, both sides know exactly who they're dealing with, can talk safely, and share one record no one can change.

ObjectionsThe hard questions, answered plainly

QuestionAnswer
Is this surveillance?No new cameras and no tracking. Location is shared only during an active stop or alert, and recordings stay on the device that made them.
What if a driver has no smartphone?The stop proceeds as it does today. The app is voluntary, and the officer terminal works from the license plate.
Who owns the data?The agency, under the advisory board's rules and state law. Every query is logged.
What does it cost?A fixed-scope pilot, then pricing per agency and per officer seat.
Does it replace CAD or body cameras?No. It works alongside them.

FundingHow a pilot gets paid for

  • A city or county pilot appropriation.
  • Byrne JAG and COPS programs.
  • NHTSA Section 402 funds through the state highway safety office.
  • State appropriations or digital ID funds.
  • A statewide cooperative contract.
  • Philanthropy to fund the independent evaluation.

EvaluationWhat we will measure and publish

Targets are agreed before launch, and a university evaluator measures them.

  • Share of stops verified on both sides.
  • Use of force and complaints, compared with a comparison division.
  • Officer safety incidents.
  • Time per stop.
  • Resident trust surveys.
  • Accessibility cases resolved without escalation.
  • Impersonation flags, and how each was investigated.
  • Evidence ledger integrity: 100% intact.
  • Adoption by drivers and officers.

Public reports at the halfway point and at the end of the pilot.

TrustOur commitments to the community

  • A community advisory board, with civil-rights, disability, youth and faith representatives.
  • A plain-language privacy promise.
  • Public reports on the results.
  • Any resident can verify the record of their own stop.
