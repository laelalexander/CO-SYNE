One network for the whole roadside

CO-SYNE is the interface and network connecting drivers, law enforcement, emergency responders and dispatch. D2ES is the AI-assisted emergency infrastructure underneath. Together they form one verified, real-time system.

Vehicle hardware

The CO-SYNE sticker

Every enrolled vehicle carries a CO-SYNE sticker. The officer scans it from the patrol car, and the stop opens with the driver's verified credential, with no plate typing and no approach needed.

  • Bumper sticker · 11.5 × 3 inSized to be read by the patrol car's camera from behind the vehicle.
  • Window cling · 4 × 4 inFor the rear window, for vehicles without a bumper location.
  • Privacy by designThe code holds only a link. Personal data is never printed on the vehicle.

Tulsa, Oklahoma

The Tulsa pilot

CO-SYNE's pilot runs on Tulsa geography, with urban and rural coverage through Tulsa 911 and north Tulsa County.

  • Urban and ruralA metro dispatch center and a rural county center, each with its own units. Routing picks the nearest center and adjusts ETAs for rural roads.
  • Local issuersPolice and sheriff's office issue officer credentials; the state issues driver credentials, all in the trust registry.
  • Oklahoma violationseCitations use Oklahoma Title 47 violations. Fines and payments in the pilot are sample values.
