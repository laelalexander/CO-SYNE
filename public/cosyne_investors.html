1 · OverviewSafer roadside stops, built for the South

A go-to-market plan for state and local agencies across the Southern United States.

CO-SYNE gives every roadside stop a verified start: the driver knows the officer is real and on duty, the officer knows who is in the car and how best to communicate, and both share one record that no one can quietly change.

2 · The problemEvery stop starts with uncertainty on both sides of the window

  • Drivers can't verify the officer. There is no reliable way, in the moment, to confirm that the person approaching is a real officer who is on duty.
  • Officers approach blind. They don't know who is inside, or whether the driver has a disability or language need that changes how to communicate.
  • Records are fragmented. Paper citations, radio traffic and separate systems leave gaps, and gaps erode trust after the fact.

3 · The platformOne verified handshake connects driver, officer and dispatch

ComponentWhat it does
Driver appStop alert, verified officer card, video or text with the officer, eCitation and payment.
Officer terminalQR sticker or plate scan, stop workflow, eCitations and reports.
DispatchLive map, drop-in three-way video, stop history.
Main AdminAgencies, devices, credentials and the signed, tamper-evident ledger.

Credentials are Ed25519 keys bound to each device; every event is written to a hash-chained, signed ledger. The apps install on phones, laptops and desktops.

4 · Where we areBuilt, live and demonstrable today

  • Three apps and the Main Admin are live at iobeacons.com/cosyne and installable from Downloads.
  • An end-to-end stop works in demonstration: sticker scan, mutual verification, video, eCitation, sealed report and dispatch oversight.
  • The Tulsa, Oklahoma pilot is in preparation.
  • The state and local go-to-market plan is drafted: read the market strategy.

Records checks and payments are simulated until state integrations are in place.

5 · Why the SouthLong corridors, growing states, a culture of regional cooperation

Southern states are growing fast, and long interstate corridors (I-10, I-20, I-35, I-40, I-75 and I-95) carry drivers across agency and state lines every day. The region has a strong culture of cooperation among sheriffs, state public safety departments and regional associations.

A credential that works across state lines is worth more on a corridor. Our home base is Oklahoma, with the Tulsa pilot as the reference deployment.

9 · Business modelAgencies subscribe. Drivers are free.

  • Officer seats for patrol terminals.
  • Agency license for Dispatch and the Main Admin.
  • State partnership for the credential and statewide deployment.
  • Onboarding and integration services.

Pricing will be validated in the Tulsa pilot. Pricing available to qualified investors on request

10 · FundingHow agencies pay

  • NHTSA highway safety grants, administered through each state's highway safety office.
  • DOJ COPS and Byrne JAG programs.
  • State appropriations.
  • Cooperative purchasing contracts.

Eligibility is confirmed program by program.
