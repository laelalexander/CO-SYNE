The programYour state issues it. CO-SYNE runs it.

CO-SYNE is a voluntary, opt-in program. A resident applies, your agency confirms them against the REAL ID-compliant license already on file, and the state issues a signed credential and a QR sticker set for their vehicle.

At a traffic stop, the officer scans the sticker from the patrol car. The driver's phone shows the officer's verified identity and the reason for the stop before anyone approaches. The officer sees a state-verified driver and vehicle, plus any accessibility notes the resident chose to add.

The system is built and running: the State Issuer Console, the Driver app, the Officer terminal and the Dispatch console are live in demonstration at iobeacons.com/cosyne.

For residents: $24.99 a year. For the state: 38% of every fee, a ready system, and no new database of residents.

The resident's pathFive steps from application to a verified stop

  1. Apply in the CO-SYNE Driver app, online, or at a DMV office. The resident enters their license number, plate and mailing address, and authorizes the $24.99 fee.
  2. Verify: your agency confirms the applicant against the REAL ID-compliant license on file. Only REAL ID-verified residents can be approved.
  3. Approve and charge: the fee is charged only on approval. A declined applicant is never charged.
  4. Issue: the state signs the credential, and the approval letter, sticker set and plate tag are printed and mailed.
  5. Activate: the resident opens the Driver app, or enters the one-time code from their letter, and the credential is bound to their phone.

The systemThe State Issuer Console

One web console for your program staff, signed in with a state access code and locked after repeated failed attempts.

  • Application queue: Submitted, Identity verified, Approved, Issued, Active or Declined, with search, filters and a full history on each file.
  • REAL ID check on every applicant before approval. Simulated in the pilot until it connects to your records system.
  • Print center: approval letters, enclosure guides and sticker sets, printed one at a time or in batches, then marked as mailed.
  • Renewals: memberships due in the next 60 days, with reminders sent 30 and 7 days before expiry.
  • Revoke and reissue: for a lost sticker, a new plate or a new vehicle. Reissuing cancels the old QR codes immediately.
  • Revenue: the state share and administration by month and by county, exported to CSV. The ledger holds amounts only, never personal details.
  • Letterhead settings: department name, office address, phone, website and signer, previewed before printing.

Within the REAL ID systemIt builds on the verification your state already did

Your agency has already verified identity, lawful status, Social Security number and residency for every REAL ID-compliant license. CO-SYNE reuses that result instead of creating a new identity process.

  • Eligibility: enrollment requires a current REAL ID-compliant license or ID on file with the state.
  • A yes or no, not a copy: the console asks your records system whether the applicant's name, date of birth and license number match a REAL ID record. CO-SYNE does not copy your license database.
  • State-signed: the credential is signed by your state's own issuer key and expires with the membership.

CO-SYNE is designed to work alongside REAL ID. It is not endorsed or approved by the U.S. Department of Homeland Security or any state, and it does not replace a driver license or REAL ID.

For residentsWhat a resident gets for $24.99 a year

  • Know who is stopping you: the officer's verified name, badge, agency and reason, before anyone approaches.
  • Stay in your car: talk with the officer by video or text from your seat.
  • Accessibility up front: a Deaf driver, a driver with autism or diabetes, or one who speaks another language can tell the officer before the approach.
  • One-tap emergency: location and vehicle go to 911 and the nearest responders.
  • Digital records: citations and the stop record on the phone, sealed so nothing can be quietly changed.
  • Works across participating states on the same sticker and app.

For the stateWhat the state gets

  • A new revenue line: 38% of every annual fee, $9.50 per enrollment, with no build cost.
  • Safer stops for troopers and local officers, with the driver verified before the approach.
  • Public trust you can show: a verified, recorded, accessible stop for every enrolled resident.
  • More value from REAL ID: a reason for residents to upgrade, and use for the verification already done.
  • No new resident database: your records stay where they are.
  • A ready system: the console, apps, printing and payments are built. Your staff run the program; CO-SYNE administers it.

Interstate trust registryBuilt to scale across state lines

Each state signs its own credentials. There is no national database.

CO-SYNE publishes a trust registry: each participating state's issuer and its public key. An officer's terminal in one state checks a driver credential signed by another state against that registry, offline if needed, and sees which state issued it.

  • Adding a state is adding an issuer to the registry, not merging records.
  • Each state keeps control: it can revoke its own residents' credentials at any time.
  • Drivers on the interstate are verified the same way at every participating state line.

Running today with Oklahoma and Texas demonstration issuers.

Federal and tribal partnersThe same model for DHS and other agencies

Because any trusted authority can be added as an issuer or a verifier, the model extends beyond state DMVs:

  • Federal agencies, such as components of the Department of Homeland Security, could verify credentials at federal facilities and checkpoints, or issue credentials for federal fleets.
  • Tribal nations can issue credentials for their residents and law enforcement.
  • Federal and tribal law enforcement can verify any participating state's credentials on the same terminal.

These are possible partnerships. No federal or tribal agency has endorsed or adopted CO-SYNE.

Privacy and securitySafeguards built in

  • Voluntary: residents opt in and can leave at any time. Stops proceed as they do today without it.
  • Nothing personal on the vehicle: the QR code holds only a link.
  • Device-bound keys: each credential is tied to a key that never leaves the resident's phone.
  • Location only when it matters: shared during an active stop or an emergency alert the resident sends.
  • A tamper-evident ledger: every issuance, payment, stop and revocation is hash-chained and signed. Payment entries hold amounts and county, not names.
  • Instant revocation: revoked or expired credentials fail verification on every terminal.
